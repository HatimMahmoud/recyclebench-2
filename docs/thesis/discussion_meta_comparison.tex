\subsection{Relation to the developer's published safety results}
\label{sec:disc-meta}

Meta reports safety results for every Llama generation we study: Llama~2-Chat in \citet{touvron2023llama2} and
Llama~3.1 in \citet{dubey2024llama3}, whose results are for the 3.1 models throughout. Llama~3 itself (April 2024)
has no technical report. Our anchor pair, Llama-3-8B-Instruct $\rightarrow$ Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, therefore spans
exactly the post-training described in \citet[\S5.4.3]{dubey2024llama3}. None of the developer's figures is
directly comparable to ours. Its safety benchmarks are internal and anonymised and, by its own account, ``not
reproducible externally'' \citep[fn.~13]{dubey2024llama3}. They report violation and false-refusal rates, mainly for
the 70B and 405B models, without verified intent preservation or judge-error correction. Table~\ref{tab:meta-corr}
therefore sets each published claim against the measurement of ours that bears on it, and grades the relation instead
of comparing numbers. Table~\ref{tab:sorry-ext} gives the one external check of our screening that is possible: the
independently published SORRY-Bench fulfilment rates \citep{xie2025sorrybench}.

Three points follow from Table~\ref{tab:meta-corr}. First, no measurement contradicts a published claim. Where the two
overlap, on the generational shift in refusal rates, the weakness of known single-turn templates, and the scale
dependence of capability-gated attacks, they agree. The independent screening check agrees as well
(Table~\ref{tab:sorry-ext}, within 1.6 points for two of three models; the 13B gap is plausibly due to decoding and
quantisation). Second, the developer evaluates safety against attack \emph{forms}: adversarial and borderline prompts,
red-team techniques, a system-level guard. It does not evaluate invariance of a refusal under rewording that keeps the
request the same, and that is the axis on which our results diverge most. The Llama-3.1 fixes that resist every tested
jailbreak template still re-open under a benign register or paraphrase change in about half of cases. Third, the
lineage trend in PBR is benchmark-dependent and so does not settle the developer's ``better balance'' claim either
way. On SORRY-Bench, whose categories include borderline requests, lower false refusal in newer generations
coincides with refusals that are less robust to surface form. On HarmBench the newer generations' refusals are, if
anything, slightly more robust. Where the fixes do re-open, the white-box probe locates the change before the first
output token: the re-opening rewording sits lower on the newer model's refusal direction. The data-recency and refusal-wording rows are hypotheses that the pre-registered held-out
replication and the human judge audit are designed to test. They are not findings.
